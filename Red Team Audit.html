Design red-team · comphy-lab.org

Good physics, underdressed.

The site does the research justice in content but not in craft. The bones are modern — Jekyll + fluid typography, a real theme system, a command palette — but the surfaces are louder than the ideas sitting on them. This audit is the candid version: what reads as amateur, what's actually fine, and the twelve moves (ranked by effort) that would make the lab's home page feel as considered as sl25.

A

What's actually hurting you

Two things a first-time visitor notices in the first five seconds. Both are fixable in a day.

A1

The homepage opens on a giant gradient word, not a sentence

Critical

What a first-time visitor sees

"Computational Multiphase Physics Laboratory" rendered enormous in a four-stop red-purple-violet gradient. There is no subtitle, no one-sentence description of what you study, no named PI above the fold. A cold visitor from a Google Scholar click has to scroll to learn that you do non-Newtonian free-surface flows and soft-matter singularities.

The hero is doing pure brand work — and the brand work is a rainbow. Every strong research-group homepage I respect (Brenner, Lohse, Stone, Bhamla, Cohen) leads with a sentence, not a logotype.

Fix

  • Keep the italic Cormorant, shrink it by 30%, add a specific H2 underneath: "We study non-Newtonian free-surface flows and soft-matter singularities."
  • Constrain the gradient to one noun, not the whole phrase. Clip it into "CoMPhy Lab" only; keep the rest in --fg-strong.
  • Above-the-fold should answer three questions in one glance: who, what, where. Durham + Vatsal's name belong on screen one, not in the footer.
A2

The footer is a dark slab that breaks the paper aesthetic

Critical

What's wrong

The footer drops to a near-black band with four white-PNG partner logos, centred copyright text, and five huge icon links. It looks like a template footer bolted onto a warm site — the purple glass header up top promises one thing and the footer delivers a different one. The Basilisk logo specifically has visible white pixels around it that bleed onto the dark band.

Fix

  • Footer background should be --c-paper-tint (#ebe5da), not black. Let the partner marks live on paper.
  • Ship transparent-background PNGs for every partner (the repo already has logoBasilisk_TransparentBackground.png — audit confirms the masked version is the one being used, and it still has edge halos).
  • Strip the five social icons at 2.5em. Put them in the header nav where they already are. Footer gets copyright + "Edit this page" + one thin rule. Silence it.
B

Craft gaps that compound

Five issues that make the site feel like a Jekyll theme you bought instead of a lab you built.

B1

Four brand colours, no hierarchy between them

High

Observation

Purple (#68236d), blue (#0056b3), coral (#cf4900), violet (#4c6ef5), red (#ff6b6b) all appear on the homepage within 800px of scroll — H1 is purple, H2 is blue, tags are coral, the hero gradient adds two more. Nothing is the interactive colour: links are blue, buttons are blue, hover is… also blue. A reader has no idea what's clickable because everything looks clickable.

Fix

  • Pick one interactive accent and defend it. The sl25 teal (#254c4a light / #6ac2bd dark) is already in your own ecosystem — use it.
  • Demote purple/blue/coral to content emphasis only (H1, strong, published-in badge). They should never be the thing you click.
  • Reserve the four-stop gradient for one element per page. Currently it shows up in three.
B2

The research page is a CV, not a research page

High

Observation

_research/index.md is a single 34 KB markdown file of chronological papers with tag pills. There are no research themes, no figures, no "what we're currently working on," no visual abstracts. A prospective PhD student landing here gets a bibliography. At a glance it reads identically to a Google Scholar page — except Scholar at least has citation counts.

Fix

  • Split into 3–4 research themes (drop impact · bubble bursting · non-Newtonian · DNS methods). Each theme is a card with one hero figure and a 2-sentence framing.
  • Under each theme, the 3 most important papers — not the 15 tagged ones. Use the paper-card component from the design system with a real thumbnail, not a gradient placeholder.
  • Add a "Currently working on" section at top. Three sentences. Updated quarterly. This is the single highest-ROI addition you can make for recruiting.
B3

Typography is doing three jobs with one font family

High

Observation

Cormorant Garamond is loaded site-wide with six weights, but outside the hero it's only used for a handful of headings. Body text falls through to system-ui, so you end up with one beautifully lettered hero sitting on top of whatever sans-serif the visitor's OS happens to ship with (San Francisco, Segoe, Roboto, Ubuntu — four different voices on four different OSes). The page has no typographic personality below the fold.

Fix

  • Commit to three families, one job each: Fraunces (or Source Serif 4) for all H2-H4 so headings have character, IBM Plex Sans for body/UI, IBM Plex Mono for code/DOIs. Keep Cormorant only for the hero H1.
  • Drop four of Cormorant's six weights. You use 500-italic and 600. That's it.
  • Use opsz variable axes — headings read at 36–48, body at 14. The same font should feel different at those sizes.
B4

News items have no narrative

High

Observation

News.md is raw markdown bullets: year → month → "Jana, S., Kolinski J., Lohse D., Sanjay V. Impacting spheres…" with a DOI. Every item reads identically. An accepted paper, a talk at APS, a new PhD joining, a media feature — all of them collapse to one-line bullets. The most human thing happening in the lab (announcements) looks like the most robotic thing on the site.

Fix

  • Three news kinds with different templates: paper (cover figure + authors + journal badge), talk (location + date + slides link), people (photo + role + welcome paragraph).
  • Use the .news-row component from the design system — purple date tile on the left, kicker + 2-3 sentences on the right. Scannable in 30 seconds.
  • Most recent 5 on the homepage, full archive on /news. Right now the homepage shows a generic intro blurb instead.
B5

Team page is a long scroll, not a roster

High

Observation

_team/index.md uses inline style="font-size: 1.1em" and hand-rolled HTML for each person. No consistent photo crop, no consistent bio length (Vatsal's section is ~400 words, others are ~30), no quick way to email or find someone's Scholar page. Collaborators and alumni are mixed in with present team.

Fix

  • Three sections, hard-separated: Present team · Collaborators · Alumni. Grid of .team-tile cards with identical photo crop (square, 240px, warm bg) and identical 2-sentence bios.
  • Each card: photo · name · role · one sentence · inline icons for email / Scholar / site. Click name → long bio page for whoever wants depth.
  • The PI's 400-word section becomes its own /people/vatsal-sanjay/, linked from the tile.
C

Polish that separates a real lab site from a template

Four things that won't get you cited but will make the site feel like someone who cares actually owns it.

C1

Dark mode is a colour swap, not a designed state

Medium

Observation

In dark mode the paper-warm palette becomes pure black, shadows disappear, and the gradient hero blows out (red + violet stops clip against dark background). The partner logos in the footer look fine in dark because they were white-PNGs to begin with — but they look wrong in light mode where they need inversion.

Fix

  • Dark paper is #12100d, not #000. Carry the warmth through.
  • Ship two versions of any photo/figure or use prefers-color-scheme media queries for figures. DNS snapshots usually look better dark.
  • Partner logos: use filter: invert(1) on light, leave alone on dark. Pick the transparent-bg version for both.
C2

No visual content above the fold on any page

Medium

Observation

You study visually stunning physics — Worthington jets, bursting bubbles, drop impact, soft-matter singularities. Your homepage has zero images above the fold. Your research page has zero figures. The Bubbles.jpg in featured/ is a gorgeous Worthington-jet photo that's not used on the landing page.

Fix

  • Hero gets a full-bleed looping MP4 of a drop impact or bubble burst (10-15 sec, poster frame prerendered). Use the .bento-hero pattern — big hero + 2 stacked metric cards right.
  • Every paper-card must have a real thumbnail. Gradient placeholders are a "todo" you ship; replace them.
  • Each research theme card needs one signature figure. This is the single biggest visual upgrade you can make.
C3

The command palette is a power-user toy with nothing to command

Medium

Observation

⌘K opens a search/command palette that has… the same 6 nav links that are already visible in the header. It's a feature looking for a reason. Meanwhile there's no full-text search across papers or news.

Fix

  • Make it useful: index every paper title, every news item, every team member. ⌘K should get me to "Sanjay 2025 PRL" in 3 keystrokes.
  • Add quick actions: "copy BibTeX", "open Scholar", "go to arXiv preprint". Palettes live and die on whether they beat scrolling.
  • Or remove it. A half-built palette is worse than none.
C4

Spacing is tight, density is high

Medium

Observation

Section padding runs ~40px, reading paragraphs hit ~75ch, H2s sit ~12px from the paragraph below. The site has things to say but is whispering them into the neighbour's ear. Compare sl25 which breathes at ~96px between sections and 68ch measures.

Fix

  • Section-to-section gap: --s-9 (96px), not 40px.
  • Reading measure: max-width: 68ch, never wider.
  • H2 gets margin-top: 2em and margin-bottom: 0.6em. Paragraphs do not start immediately below headings.
  • Line-height 1.65 for body, not 1.5. Academic text needs air.
D

What you should absolutely keep

Not everything is broken. These three moves are legitimately better than 90% of group sites.

D1

Cormorant Garamond italic for the lab name

Keep

Why it works

An italic display serif on a scholarly site is a strong, specific choice. It carries warmth and literary connotation (Cormorant is explicitly modelled on Garamond — a book-face lineage). Very few lab sites use anything other than Inter. This is signature.

Just

  • Use it exactly once per page.
  • Load only italic 500 and italic 600. Drop the other 10 weights.
  • Pair it with Fraunces / Source Serif for H2s so it doesn't feel orphaned.
D2

The light/dark toggle architecture

Keep

Why it works

Inline theme-init.html runs before paint, reads localStorage, falls back to prefers-color-scheme. No flash, no layout shift. The dispatch of a themeChange CustomEvent so other scripts can react is genuinely thoughtful — most sites either ignore preference or flash white on load. This is craft.

D3

Lucida-style translucent glass header

Keep

Why it works

The header uses a lightly purple-tinted glass (backdrop-filter: blur(8px) on rgba(104,35,109,0.08)) which gives the page its single strongest hit of brand identity without being loud. Icons for Scholar, GitHub, Bluesky all live there. Don't touch it beyond swapping the ⌘K button for something real.

Here's the honest summary. The site's ideas are ambitious — gradient hero, command palette, real theme system, fluid typography, Cormorant italic wordmark — and the execution on each one is 70%. None of them are wrong. All of them need one more editing pass. The top three moves above are genuinely transformational and together take less than a week; the rest is incremental. Your sl25 site is already the refined direction; bring that editor's eye home.
